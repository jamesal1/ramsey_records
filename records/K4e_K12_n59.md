## How it was found

Seed: the disjoint union of Van Overberghe's 48-vertex (K4-e, K10)-graph `J4K10n48` and a 6-vertex graph with no K4-e whose complement is triangle-free, a 54-vertex (K4-e, K12)-graph. The complement of a disjoint union is the join of the complements, so the largest clique (or clique minus an edge) in the complement of a union can be read off from the parts.

A *SAT step* is an exact search (CaDiCaL through PySAT) that deletes up to d vertices of the current graph and adds d + 1 new ones, keeping every other vertex and edge. Edges at the new vertices are the variables; every forbidden subgraph in a candidate is blocked by a new clause, and the search either returns a graph one vertex larger or proves that no graph of that form exists.

Steps with d ≤ 3 climbed from 54 to 59 vertices on the VM; only added vertices were deleted, so vertices 0–47 are `J4K10n48` unchanged. The d ≤ 3 step on the 59-vertex graph is UNSAT (31 min). The same ladder started from Van Overberghe's own 57-vertex (K4-e, K12)-graph was UNSAT at d ≤ 3 within 2 minutes.

DS1.18 lists a construction of Jiang, Liang, Sun and Xu (its 3.1(k)) as R(K4-e, K_{m+n+1}) ≥ R(3, m+1) + R(3, n+1) + n. The published paper (Graphs Combin. 37 (2021) 651–661) ends in + m, the smaller index. The misprinted form would give 60 (a tie) here; the correct form gives only 51.
