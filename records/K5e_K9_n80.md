## How it was found

After the extension ladders stalled at 79 vertices, the search switched to structured graphs. A Cayley graph on a group G is given by an inverse-closed connection set S: x ~ y exactly when x⁻¹y is in S. The search makes membership of each inverse pair in S a SAT variable and adds a blocking clause for every forbidden subgraph found in G or its complement, until the solver either returns a valid S or proves none exists.

A sweep over abelian groups of order 80 found a connection set on Z4 × Z4 × Z5 in about a second. Z2⁴ × Z5 and Z4 × Z2² × Z5 also work at 80 vertices. Because K10-e contains K9, the graph also proves R(K5-e, K10-e) ≥ 81.
