## How it was found

A Cayley graph on a group G is given by an inverse-closed connection set S: x ~ y exactly when x⁻¹y is in S. The search makes membership of each inverse pair in S a SAT variable and adds a blocking clause for every forbidden subgraph found in G or its complement, until the solver either returns a valid S or proves none exists.

A staged SAT sweep over non-abelian groups first gave 84-vertex (K5-e, K10-e)-graphs on SmallGroup(84,10) and SmallGroup(84,11) (R ≥ 85), then this 96-vertex graph on SmallGroup(96,12): 33-regular with 5568 triangles. The same sweep found two 96-vertex (K5-e, K10)-graphs on SmallGroup(96,12) and SmallGroup(96,13), not isomorphic to this one or to each other; they prove nothing more, so they are not published here.

Because K10 contains K10-e, this graph also proves R(K5-e, K10) ≥ 97. Adding an isolated vertex gives a 97-vertex (K5-e, K11-e)-graph, the seed of [the 100-vertex record](K5e_K11e_n100.md).
