## How it was found

A Cayley graph on a group G is given by an inverse-closed connection set S: x ~ y exactly when x⁻¹y is in S. The search makes membership of each inverse pair in S a SAT variable and adds a blocking clause for every forbidden subgraph found in G or its complement, until the solver either returns a valid S or proves none exists.

Orbit restriction: S is required to be a union of orbits of the multiplier x ↦ 31x (order 6) on Z16 × Z2 × Z7 (≅ Z112 × Z2), which leaves 49 orbit variables. A sweep over abelian groups of orders 211–256 on a 64-core VM solved this case in 176 s. The graph is 83-regular.

The same graph also proves R(K6-e, K12), R(K5, K13-e) and R(K6-e, K13-e) ≥ 225 by containment; those are not claimed separately here.
