## How it was found

Seed: [the 96-vertex (K5-e, K10-e) Cayley graph](K5e_K10e_n96.md) plus one isolated vertex. In the complement the new vertex is joined to everything, so a K11-e there would need a K10-e among the other vertices; the 97-vertex seed is therefore a (K5-e, K11-e)-graph.

A *SAT step* is an exact search (CaDiCaL through PySAT) that deletes up to d vertices of the current graph and adds d + 1 new ones, keeping every other vertex and edge. Edges at the new vertices are the variables; every forbidden subgraph in a candidate is blocked by a new clause, and the search either returns a graph one vertex larger or proves that no graph of that form exists.

Three steps with d ≤ 1 (1–1.5 min each) climbed from 97 to 100 vertices. Only added vertices were deleted, so vertices 0–95 are the Cayley graph unchanged. On the 100-vertex graph the d ≤ 1 step is UNSAT and d ≤ 2 ran out of its 1 h budget. Extensions that keep a coset structure over SmallGroup(96,12) were also tried: every case with a nontrivial point stabiliser is UNSAT (98–120 vertices) and regular orbits of size 6 are UNSAT (102 vertices).
