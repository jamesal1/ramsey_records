## How it was found

A Cayley graph on a group G is given by an inverse-closed connection set S: x ~ y exactly when x⁻¹y is in S. The search makes membership of each inverse pair in S a SAT variable and adds a blocking clause for every forbidden subgraph found in G or its complement, until the solver either returns a valid S or proves none exists.

Orbit restriction: S is required to be a union of orbits of a group of multipliers x ↦ ax. With the multiplier group generated by 7 (order 28) the circulant on Z145 has only 5 orbit variables, and the solver found this 70-regular graph in 6 s. The multiplier groups generated by 4, 6 and 28 give the same graph. Because K7-e contains K6, it also proves R(K7-e, K9-e) ≥ 146.
